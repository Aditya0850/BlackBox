

> **Take messy crime data → connect the dots → build a criminal network graph → use AI to find important/suspicious connections → show investigators what matters.**

Let’s break the entire thing down.

## 1. What problem are they actually talking about?

Imagine police have information about **100 suspects**.

But the information is scattered:

* FIR says **A met B**
* CDR says **A called C**
* Bank records say **C sent ₹50,000 to D**
* Surveillance says **D was seen with E**
* Social media says **B follows E**
* Another report says **E owns a vehicle registered to F**
* Criminal database says **F was previously associated with G**

A human investigator has to manually connect all of this.

That's where things get difficult.

The important relationship might be:

**A → B → C → D → E → F**

But that connection is buried across thousands of records.

---

# 2. What does "criminal network" mean?

Think of it like a **social network**, except instead of friends, you have suspects, locations, phones, vehicles, money, organizations, etc.

For example:

```text
             [Person B]
             /        \
            /          \
     [Person A]       [Phone X]
          |               |
       [Vehicle]       [Person C]
                          |
                       [Bank]
                          |
                       [Person D]
```

Here:

* **Person** = Entity
* **Phone** = Entity
* **Bank account** = Entity
* **Vehicle** = Entity
* **Connection** = Relationship

This is called a **graph/network**.

---

# 3. What data goes into the system?

The statement gives you several sources.

### FIRs / Police Reports

Mostly unstructured text.

Example:

> "Rahul Sharma met Amit Kumar near Park Street on 14 August."

AI needs to extract:

```text
Person: Rahul Sharma
Person: Amit Kumar
Location: Park Street
Date: 14 August
Relationship: met
```

This is where **NLP** comes in.

---

### CDRs

Call Detail Records.

Example:

```text
A → B
Call duration: 18 minutes
Date: 14 Aug
Time: 22:13
```

The system can discover:

> A and B communicate frequently.

Important: CDR generally gives metadata, **not the actual conversation content**.

---

### Financial records

Example:

```text
A → ₹50,000 → B
B → ₹2,00,000 → C
```

The system can discover unusual financial relationships.

---

### Surveillance reports

Example:

> "Person A was observed entering Location X with Person B."

Now:

```text
A ──seen_with── B
A ──visited── Location X
```

---

### Social media intelligence

Potentially:

```text
A follows B
A interacts with B
A appears in photo with B
```

But this needs careful handling because **association ≠ criminal involvement**.

That's a very important point for your project.

---

# 4. What does AI actually do?

The AI isn't supposed to say:

> "This person is a criminal."

That's dangerous and also **not what the problem statement asks for**.

Instead:

> **AI finds relationships and patterns that investigators may have missed.**

For example:

```text
Person A
 ↓
12 calls
 ↓
Person B
 ↓
₹8 lakh transaction
 ↓
Person C
 ↓
same location
 ↓
Person D
```

The system could tell investigators:

> **"A, B, C and D form a highly connected cluster based on communication, financial and location relationships."**

Then the police investigate it.

---

# 5. What does "entity extraction" mean?

This is one of the most important parts.

Suppose the report says:

> "Rohit met Sameer at Kolkata Airport using a white Toyota Fortuner."

AI extracts:

| Entity          | Type     |
| --------------- | -------- |
| Rohit           | Person   |
| Sameer          | Person   |
| Kolkata Airport | Location |
| Toyota Fortuner | Vehicle  |

Then relationships:

```text
Rohit ──met── Sameer
Rohit ──visited── Kolkata Airport
Rohit ──used── Toyota Fortuner
```

This is basically **NLP + Named Entity Recognition (NER)**.

---

# 6. Then comes the BIG part: Graph construction

All those extracted entities go into a graph.

Imagine:

```text
             Person A
            /   |    \
         call  meet  money
          /     |      \
    Person B  Person C  Person D
        |          |
     vehicle     location
```

Now you have a machine-readable representation of the entire network.

This is where technologies such as **Neo4j / graph databases / NetworkX** can become useful.

---

# 7. "Identify key individuals"

This means:

> Who are the most important nodes in the network?

Suppose:

```text
A ─ B ─ C ─ D
    │   │
    E   F
        │
        G
```

Maybe **C** isn't the person with the most connections, but C connects two otherwise separate groups.

That can be extremely interesting.

Graph algorithms can calculate things like:

### Degree centrality

Who has the most direct connections?

### Betweenness centrality

Who acts as a **bridge** between different groups?

### PageRank / influence-style scoring

Which nodes are structurally important?

So instead of:

> "Person C is guilty."

You say:

> **"Person C has unusually high network centrality and connects multiple clusters."**

Much safer and more useful.

---

# 8. "Detect suspicious patterns"

This is the ML/anomaly-detection part.

Example:

Normally:

```text
A → B
₹5,000
₹10,000
₹8,000
```

Suddenly:

```text
A → B
₹9,80,000
```

That's an **anomaly**.

Another example:

```text
Person A
↓
Location X
↓
Person B
↓
Location Y
↓
Person C
```

And this pattern repeatedly occurs shortly before known criminal incidents.

The system can flag it for investigation.

Again:

**Flag ≠ proof of crime.**

---

# 9. What does "actionable intelligence" mean?

This sounds fancy but means:

> **Give investigators information they can actually use.**

Instead of dumping 50,000 records on a police officer:

```text
50,000 records
      ↓
AI analysis
      ↓
Important entities
      ↓
Important relationships
      ↓
Suspicious patterns
      ↓
Network visualization
      ↓
Investigator
```

The investigator can then explore the network.

## 12. The most important interpretation

This isn't really:

> **"Build an AI that catches criminals."**

It's:

> **"Build an intelligence-analysis system that converts fragmented crime data into a connected, explorable network and highlights relationships/patterns worth investigating."**

And that's actually a **much stronger and more defensible project**.

The killer feature isn't the chatbot.

It's the **network intelligence engine underneath it**.
